# the-conduct-of-magic

A shop-management, inspection and crafting game about running an enchanter's shop, made in Godot. Serve customers and inspect their goods by day, then enchant items by night.

Game design: [docs/design/game-design.md](docs/design/game-design.md)

Story: [docs/story/story-bible.md](docs/story/story-bible.md)

## Running the inscribing prototype
Open the project in Godot 4.7 and run it (F5). The main scene is the inscribing table (`inscribing/inscribing_table.tscn`), set up with the demo player in `inscribing/demo/demo_setup.tres`: three swords of different sizes (a dagger, a short sword and a long sword) and a pre-carved long sword. Bastion, in the enchantment list, is an example of a big node with two ports, not a real enchantment.

- **Place an enchantment:** drag its card onto the item, pressing R or scrolling to rotate it on the way. Clicking a card holds it instead, so you can click to place.
- **Try the layouts:** the Layout dropdown at the bottom right switches between the sidebar (Tab opens and closes it), a bottom tray, a quick palette (hold Space) and the original lists.
- **Draw a line:** press on a node and drag through neighbouring cells to another node. A line fills every cell it passes, so lines can't cross; move back along the line to undo cells. Lines can join magic nodes to each other to build a chain.
- **Insert a material:** drag its card onto an enchantment node. Nodes that accept it get a green ring. A planned material leaves your inventory and is held by that item until you take it back out.
- **Take back planned things:** right click a material, a line, then an enchantment. Esc drops what you're holding or cancels a line.
- **Commit:** commits every enchantment that is fully active. Its materials are used up then.
- **Disenchant:** pick an item with carvings (Long sword #4 arrives pre-carved) and press Disenchant above the board. Each committed line shows its rune cost. Double-click a material card to dissolve it for runes, click lines to plan cutting them, then click an enchantment node to pull it out once its whole chain is cut. Every node is labelled (M4 is magic node 4, Keen B is Keen's second node), and the plan and tooltips use the same names. Your runes are in the bar above the board, the plan is on the right (a step you can't afford shows red), and right click or Undo takes back the last step. Commit pulls out the planned enchantments and frees their cells.

Tests: `godot --headless --path . res://tests/test_inscription_board.tscn`, `res://tests/test_disenchant_board.tscn`, `res://tests/test_item_overview.tscn`, `res://tests/test_scene_bake.tscn` and `res://tests/test_solvability_checker.tscn` (each exits 1 on failure).
Solvability check (dev tool): in `inscribing/inscribing_table.tscn`, select the `SolvabilityCheck` node and tick **Enabled**. Run the game (F5) and the Output panel shows, for each carved item (again whenever you pick one), whether it can be disenchanted with your materials (for all its carvings together and for each alone), what to dissolve and the steps in order, numbered as they'll appear in the plan list, like "4. Cut Kindle A – M2". Nodes go by the labels drawn on the board in Disenchant mode: **M4** is magic node 4, **Keen B** is Keen's second node (letters follow the enchantment's own node order). The report also lists what each enchantment node holds, like "Keen B (Blue stone)".
Headless: `godot --headless --path . res://tools/solvability_checker.tscn` prints the same report for the demo items; its test is `res://tests/test_solvability_checker.tscn`.
Building content in the editor: [build a map](docs/guides/build-a-map.md) (cell and magic node layers, and a tutorial for making your own magic node, with the Ember Well on the short sword as the example), [build an enchantment](docs/guides/build-an-enchantment.md) (big nodes with ports, more than one line between two nodes, and art; Bastion is the example).
Placeholder art (drawn by Claude, to be replaced) is in `art/placeholder/`; `python3 tools/placeholder_art/make_placeholder_art.py` redraws it.
The table's board is drawn with that art through `art/placeholder/board_skin.tres` (one texture per kind of board element, sheet 0006); untick **Use art** on the `InscribingTable` root node to see the flat drawing instead.

Demo content is generated by `res://tools/build_demo_content.tscn`; rerunning it overwrites the demo `.tres` files.

## Tests and demo content
The tests use frozen copies of the demo content in `tests/fixtures/` (enchantments and their scenes, materials, the swords and their map scenes, the tile set, the pre-carved sword and the demo setup), so editing the demo in the editor never breaks the checks. To change what a test checks, edit its fixture, not the demo file.
A few checks read the demo as it ships, without fixed numbers: every map and enchantment scene is baked into its shipped resource, every demo enchantment fits on the largest demo item, the demo opens on the table without errors, and every pre-carved item can be cleared with the demo materials. If one of those fails after an edit, the demo itself needs fixing (usually a scene that wasn't baked).
