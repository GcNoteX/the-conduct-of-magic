## How this bible works

All story content here is Gavin's. Claude records what Gavin designs, asks questions, points out plot holes, gives feedback against the agreed themes and brings outside references on story construction. Claude doesn't add story content of its own.

This Doc is the main copy. A mirror is kept in the repo at `docs/story/story-bible.md` and refreshed from time to time. Gameplay rules live in the game design doc (`docs/design/game-design.md`), and this bible points to it rather than repeating it. Unmarked facts are decided; **Proposed** marks ideas not yet agreed.

## Theme, tone and premise

**Theme.** The game is a discussion of one question: *if you have a god-given talent that others don't, what do you use it for?* The answers aren't limited to helping people or getting rich.

**Tone.** Cosy with a moral edge. Most of the game, and the shop above all, should feel like *Stardew Valley*. Later areas bring in a little more mystery, but the shop stays cosy.

**Premise.** A teenager with the gift of enchanting gets work experience in their uncle's workshop, learning the trade from him.

**How the story is told.** Mostly through the counter. Customers return, remember and carry their own plots, which the player's decisions move forward or cut off. The wider world reaches the player through printed matter (news, guides, adverts) and contacts. The story later breaks off into different environments, where the rest of it develops.

## Setting and magic

**Magic.** Enchanting (inscribing) is a rare talent given at birth. Most people can't do it because of how their mana behaves. Having the right mana isn't enough either: not everyone with the right attributes has the proper mindset or the talent for inscribing.

**The uncle's town.** Not yet defined. The design doc says it must hold an enchanters' guild, a town authority that sets laws, a bookstore, a travelling book peddler, and the uncle's store. It also needs enough factions that helping one can break another's rules.

**The wider world.** Not yet defined. The story later moves into different environments. The design doc names three possible destinations: the University, the Adventurers' Guild and the Black Market.

**Culture.** The town has no culture fusion. The East Asian reference only describes the uncle's generosity toward family.

## Characters

### The player

- A teenager doing work experience in the uncle's workshop.
- Born with the gift, and quite talented compared with others who have it.
- Name and background not yet defined.

### The uncle

- Can inscribe. He's the player's mentor, and an honest worker.
- Kind and generous, taking care of the player as family.
- Owns the shop and settles the books at the end of each day.
- Will recommend the player to a school or a workplace when the workshop chapter ends.
- In the tutorial phase he negotiates and makes the final call while the player inspects. He hands negotiation over as he comes to trust the player.
- How he responds to a bad day is still open: less pay depending on how many resources were wasted, feedback only, or a trust meter.

## Story structure

The story starts in the uncle's workshop, then breaks off into different environments where the rest of it develops.

### The uncle's workshop

- Work experience over a few days or weeks; the game progresses day to day.
- Starts as a tutorial: the player inspects and the uncle negotiates. Control hands over gradually.
- Ends with the uncle recommending the player to a school or a workplace.
- End states from the design doc: running out of money, or not having enough money for the next stage. Each leads to different endings.

### Later environments

The design doc's routes, depending on performance, money and choices:

| Route | How you get there | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| University | A near-perfect run with enough money | The respectable, scholarly path |
| Adventurers' Guild | Not enough money for the university | Practical, dangerous work |
| Black Market | **Proposed:** shady commissions or allegiances | High reward, high risk |

Each environment has its own place, mechanics and customers, and more mystery than the shop.

## Open questions

Most important first.

1. **Recommendation versus money.** The design doc sends the player to a route by money and performance, and now the uncle recommends them. Which one decides? What do the money endings mean if the uncle is the one recommending?
2. **Who voices the other answers?** The theme is a discussion, so different characters could hold different answers to what a gift is for. Which answers do you want the player to meet, and through whom?
3. **What does the uncle use his gift for?** As an honest worker, is he the game's model answer, one answer among several, or deliberately not an answer?
4. **A bad day at the counter.** Less pay depending on the resources wasted, feedback only, or a trust meter?
5. **Which realistic characters does a teenager meet here?** How many recurring customers in the workshop chapter, and how many plots run at once?
6. **Who knows about the player's talent?** Customers, the guild, the town?
7. **Harsh systems in a cosy shop.** Curses, faulty materials that blow up, money endings and Black Market deaths come from the design doc. Do they stay, soften, or move to later environments?
8. **Factions in town.** Which guilds and groups exist, and which lead to each environment?
9. **The player.** Name, exact age, where they come from. Fixed or player-defined?
10. **Mana.** Does everyone have mana? If so, what can ordinary people do with theirs?
11. **Mindset.** What does "the proper mindset" for inscribing mean in play? Can the player lose it, or does the story test it?
12. **One plot or many?** Is there an overarching plot through all environments, or is the story made of customer plots?
13. **The book peddler.** A recurring character with a story of their own?
14. **Workshop chapter length.** How many in-game days?
