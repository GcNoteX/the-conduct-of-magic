# The Conduct of Magic: Story Bible

> Living document for the story the player goes through: the world, the characters they meet, the chapters and the endings. Gameplay rules live in the [game design document](../design/game-design.md); this file links to it rather than repeating it.
> Same conventions as the design doc: **Decided** facts are stated plainly, **Proposed** marks suggestions not yet agreed, **On hold** marks parked ideas, and **Open questions** collect what still needs deciding.

## 1. Premise and theme

The main character has the **gift of enchanting** and starts out as an apprentice in their **uncle's workshop** (design doc §9.1).

**Theme.** The title is the question the game asks. You've been given a rare gift. Enchanting can help a great many people, and it can also make you rich. How you conduct yourself with it is up to you (design doc §1).

**How the story is told.** Mostly through the counter. Customers return, remember and carry their own plots, which your decisions move forward or cut off (pillar 4). The wider world reaches you through **printed matter** (news, culture guides, adverts) and **contacts**, because there's no foraging and the player rarely leaves the shop (design doc §4.2, §4.3).

## 2. Setting

### 2.1 The uncle's town
*Not yet defined.* What we know it must contain:
- An **enchanters' guild** (and possibly other guilds) setting standards, certifications and banned work.
- A **town or city authority** setting what's legal to make, sell or possess.
- A **bookstore**, and a **travelling book peddler** who visits the shop.
- The **uncle's store**, which also sells expedited materials at a surcharge.
- Enough factions that helping one can break another's rules (design doc §6).

### 2.2 The wider world
*Not yet defined.* It must hold three destinations the player can end up in: the **University**, the **Adventurers' Guild** and the **Black Market** (§4.2). Different **cultures** have their own customs, markings and goods, which culture guides teach (design doc §4.2).

### 2.3 Magic in the world
- Enchanting is done by carving patterns into an item's map of magic nodes and powering them with materials (design doc §7). How the characters themselves describe this is *not yet defined*.
- Customers can tell whether an enchantment works (a warm blade, a light, rust resistance).
- **Curses** and **faulty materials** exist and can harm the shop (design doc §5.3).

## 3. Characters

### 3.1 The main character
- Has the gift of enchanting. Young, learning the trade.
- Name, background, and why they're living with their uncle are *not yet defined*.

### 3.2 The uncle
- Owns the workshop and **settles the books at the end of each day**.
- He's the player's first source of feedback: bad commissions mean lower income that day, and he docks the player's pay.
- In the tutorial phase **he negotiates and makes the final call** while the player inspects and passes sheets to him approved or flagged. As he comes to trust the player, he hands the negotiation over (design doc §9.2.1).
- Restocks materials early on, then stops (design doc §4.4).
- His personality and whether he has an agenda of his own are *open* (Q3).

### 3.3 Recurring customers
Every customer has a story of their own. The shape of a customer arc, from the commission rules (design doc §5):
1. They arrive with a commission, possibly with their own item, materials, ID or papers.
2. You inspect and accept or reject. Accepting moves their plot forward; rejecting may cut it off or send it elsewhere.
3. They return **on a later day** to collect, and that's where most consequences land.
4. A pleased customer may bring gifts such as books, which can unlock contacts.

*No customers are written yet.* A template for each one is in §6.

### 3.4 Other people the player meets
| Who | Role | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **The book peddler** | Travelling seller who brings printed matter into the shop | Exists as a mechanic, not yet a character |
| **Contacts** | Suppliers, specialists and clients unlocked by adverts; may visit as special customers | Exist as a mechanic, none written |

### 3.5 Factions
| Faction | What we know | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Enchanters' guild** | Sets standards, certifications and banned work | Exists, not written |
| **Town authority** | Sets laws on making, selling and possessing goods | Exists, not written |
| **Other guilds** | Possible | Open |
| **Groups behind each route** | Something in town should lead to the University, Adventurers' Guild and Black Market | Open (Q6) |

## 4. Story structure

### 4.1 Chapter 1: the uncle's workshop
**Decided**
- The player works the counter under the uncle for a few days or weeks.
- Starts as a tutorial: the player inspects, the uncle negotiates with fixed deadlines. Control hands over gradually (customer-set deadlines, then the calendar pick).
- The game progresses day to day.

**End states** (design doc §9.2.2)
- **Running out of money** leads to different endings.
- **Not having enough money to progress to the next stage** also leads to different endings.
- Death or similar may come later; in this chapter the money endings are the end states.

*Not yet defined:* the chapter's length (Q4), its beats, and what event closes it.

### 4.2 Chapter 2: the route
After the workshop, the player moves on. Where depends on **performance, money and choices**:

| Route | How you get there | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **University** | A near-perfect run with enough money | The respectable, scholarly path |
| **Adventurers' Guild** | Not enough money for the university | Practical, dangerous work |
| **Black Market** | *Proposed:* shady commissions or allegiances | High reward, high risk; early deaths possible |

Each route is a **macro level** with its own place, mechanics and customers, to be developed later. Whether the player chooses or is assigned is *open* (Q5).

### 4.3 Endings
| Ending | Trigger | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Money ending(s) in the workshop | Run out of money | Decided to exist, not written |
| Can't-afford-to-progress ending(s) | Not enough money for the next stage | Decided to exist, not written |
| Route endings | Later chapters | Not defined |

## 5. Story through the systems

Places where the existing mechanics can carry story, to keep in mind when writing:
- **Rejecting a commission** is a story choice as well as a rules call.
- **Fair blame** (proposed): penalties scale with whether the problem was detectable, so a customer's lie can be their fault.
- **News** reports rule changes and story beats happening elsewhere.
- **Cursed items** can cause strange effects in the shop until shed.
- **Allegiances** decide which rules you break and which doors open.

## 6. Customer template

Copy this for each recurring customer.

```
### <Name>
- **Who they are:** one line
- **Faction / allegiance:**
- **First visit:** what they ask for, what they bring, what's suspicious
- **What's true:** what inspection can reveal
- **If accepted:** how their plot moves; what happens on collection
- **If rejected:** where their plot goes instead
- **Returns:** later visits, gifts, contacts unlocked
- **Feeds into:** which route, faction or ending this pushes toward
```

## 7. Open questions

**Most important first**
1. **Tone.** How dark and how funny is the world? Cosy shopkeeping with a moral edge, or grim and dangerous like Papers, Please?
2. **The gift.** Is enchanting rare or unique to the main character, and who knows about it? Is it something that has to be hidden?
3. **The uncle.** A kindly mentor, a stern businessman, or someone with an agenda (debts, a secret, a past with one of the factions)?
4. **Chapter 1 length and shape.** How many in-game days, and is there a story event that ends it (an exam, a letter, a crisis), or does it end on the money check alone?
5. **Choosing a route.** Does the player choose, or does the game assign it from their results? Can a rich player still pick the Black Market?
6. **Factions in town.** Which guilds and groups exist, and which of them lead to each route?

**Also open**
7. Who is the main character: name, age, where they came from, why they're with the uncle? Fixed or player-defined?
8. Where and when is this? An invented fantasy town, or something close to a historical period (the design doc already leans on medieval guilds and hallmarking)?
9. How many recurring customers in chapter 1, and how many plots run at once?
10. Is the book peddler a recurring character with a story of their own?
11. Is there an overarching plot that runs through all chapters, or is the story made of customer plots?
